## 1. What was implemented (defaults off → delivery bit-identical)

`research/gsplat_depth_loss.py` — a PGSR single-view normal–depth consistency regularizer
that needs **ONE** extra gsplat 1.5.3 pass per application:

* per-Gaussian planar normal = world-space **smallest-scale axis** `R @ e_min` (PGSR's
  normal for a flattened Gaussian; the zero PLY normals from task-12 are not used);
* the extra pass renders that normal field as *colours* in `render_mode="RGB+ED"`, so
  **one pass yields both** the alpha-composited normal map (RGB) and the expected depth
  (ED) — no second pass for the normal map;
* `n_depth = normalize(cross(Sobel_x(D), Sobel_y(D)))`; on pixels with `alpha>0.5`,
  `D>0` and finite gradients, the loss is `mean(1 − |cos(n_depth, n_render)|)`.

`research/train_sweep.py` new flags (all default off): `--depth_reg_weight`,
`--depth_reg_every` (10), `--depth_reg_from` (4000), `--depth_reg_until` (5000); the
trainer record gains `depth_reg = {weight, every, from_iter, until_iter, events:[{iteration,
seconds, valid_fraction, depth_mean, normal_cos_mean}]}`. `research/run_sweep.py` passes the
flags through; driver arms `pgsr_sv10` (every 10) and `pgsr_sv5` (every 5), weight 0.2.

**Measured per-application cost** (`cost.json`, from `research/depth_cost_probe.py` on the
bicycle delivery PLY: 993,861 Gaussians, 576×960, after warm-up):

|---|---:|

| gsplat RGB+ED forward / forward+backward | 1.986 / **7.652 ms** |

So the financial bar is: **adopt only if Δq ≥ +0.5 on both DL3DV bad cases** (or Δtotal > 0
on both), per the task's verdict rule.

Per-application cost measured from the trainer's own `depth_reg.events` (not the probe):
101 applications in 4000–5000 every 10 ⇒ **first application 0.47–0.49 s** (gsplat
backend/first-call warm-up) then **2.7 ms per application** steady-state; total event time
**0.75 s** (1-in-10) / **1.03 s** (1-in-5), i.e. the +0.77 s / +1.53 s prediction is
confirmed (the larger `stage1` deltas (+1.1…+2.3 s) include the one-time warm-up plus
run-to-run noise). **The cost is as cheap as designed and it still loses.**

The mechanism is visible and is the answer to the user's question: the regularizer
**works geometrically** — mean |cos(normal, depth-normal)| rises 0.425→0.675 (0b5635) and
0.345→0.435 (0f622ea) at w=0.2, 0.429→0.525 at w=0.02 — yet PSNR falls by up to 1.7 dB.
Forcing depth-consistent surfaces on a photometric-NVS objective removes capacity the
renderer was using to fit the images (the depth-Sobel normal field is noisy at this
resolution, and the metric has no surface term). The damage scales with the weight
(0b5635: w=0.2 → Δq −3.52, w=0.02 → −0.46, roughly linear), so there is **no positive
weight regime**: at the weight where it is harmless it is also a no-op.

## 3. Verdict

**FAIL on the task's adopt rule on both DL3DV bad cases, at every frequency and weight
tested.** At the 1-in-10 late-window point the quality deltas are **−3.52 (0b5635)** and
**−0.23 (0f622ea)** quality points for an extra B of only ~+0.8 s (TIME −0.18); 1-in-5 is
worse (−5.81 / −1.47). The low-weight ablation (−0.46 at w=0.02) rules out the "weight too
high" explanation: the effect is monotone in weight with no positive regime.

**PGSR is now closed on the objective argument as well, not only on cost.** Late-window,
low-frequency depth regularization *does* move the Gaussian normals (the geometry term is
active) but it moves photometric NVS **down**; the limiting factor is that our metric has
no surface term while the regularizer spends model capacity on depth-consistent surfaces.
Together with task-12 (representation half impossible/redundant, full-rate depth +158 % B),
the PGSR direction has no affordable and beneficial corner. No multi-view arm was run —
the single-view term shows no quality signal to build on (task rule: only if sv shows
signal).
